FAQ

Questions clients ask before hiring.

If something here isn't covered, ask me on the call. I'd rather over-explain than have you guess.

How do you price work?
Three ways, depending on what suits you. Hourly starts from $15/hr and works well for smaller tasks or open-ended work. Defined projects are quoted as a fixed price against a written scope, so you know the total before anything starts. Ongoing work runs as a monthly retainer with reserved capacity.
What are your payment terms?
For fixed-scope projects: 50% to start and 50% on delivery, with larger builds split across milestones instead. Retainers are invoiced monthly. Payment by bank transfer or Wise, in USD, EUR, or GBP.
Will you sign an NDA and a contract?
Yes to both, and I'm happy to work on your paperwork rather than mine. If you don't have a contract template, I'll supply a straightforward one covering scope, payment, confidentiality, and IP.
Who owns the code?
You do. All code and associated IP transfer to you on final payment, and I work directly in your repository from day one so there's never a handover cliff. Nothing is held hostage on my machine.
How much timezone overlap do we get?
I'm based in India (IST, UTC+5:30). That gives full overlap with European business hours, and my evenings cover US Eastern mornings, roughly 8am to 12pm ET. I schedule calls in your timezone, not mine, and async updates land before your day starts.
Can you work with our existing team and codebase?
That's a large share of what I do. I work through your normal pull request and code review process, follow the conventions already in the repo, and don't propose rewrites when a targeted fix will do. Legacy Laravel and Symfony codebases are familiar territory.
What happens if you become unavailable?
Everything lives in your repository with documentation and a deployment runbook kept current as we go, so another developer can pick up the work without archaeology. Retainers carry 30 days notice on both sides.
How soon can you start?
Usually within one to two weeks, occasionally sooner for audits. The scoping call is the fastest way to find out, and I'll tell you honestly if my next slot is further out than you need.
